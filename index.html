    Battle-Tested Scope Defense Email Scripts

    Word-for-word tactical responses for turning out-of-scope requests into paid opportunities without alienating the client.

    Scenario 1

    "Can you just quickly add this small feature?"

    Use when a client slips in extra deliverables mid-project under the guise of a "quick tweak".

    Hi [Client Name], Thanks for sending this over! That feature would definitely add great value to the project. Because this falls outside our original Statement of Work (which is focused on [Core Deliverable]), we have two straightforward paths forward: 1. **Include it in Phase 2:** We keep our current launch date intact, finish the agreed deliverables, and queue this up immediately as our next sprint. 2. **Execute a Change Order now:** We can add this into our current milestone right away for an additional [Amount, e.g. $650] and a [Number, e.g. 3-day] timeline extension. Let me know which option aligns best with your launch schedule, and I'll send over the details! Best regards, [Your Name]
    Scenario 2

    "We've exceeded the agreed revision limit"

    Use when a client requests round 4 or 5 of feedback when the contract specified 2 rounds.

    Hi [Client Name], I've reviewed the latest feedback round. We've now completed the [Number, e.g. 2] revision rounds included in our project agreement. To make sure we get every remaining refinement exactly right without delaying the final release, I can execute these additional edits at our standard hourly rate of [$XX/hr] (estimated at [X] hours), or as a fixed refinement sprint for [$XXX]. Please let me know if you'd like me to proceed with these updates under this arrangement! Best regards, [Your Name]
    Scenario 3

    "A new stakeholder joined and wants changes"

    Use when a new executive or manager enters mid-project and tries to overhaul approved work.

    Hi [Client Name], It's great to have [Stakeholder Name]'s input on the project. Since the previous milestone was formally approved on [Date], incorporating these new directional changes will require revisiting finalized architecture. I've drafted a streamlined Change Order outlining the required hours ([X] hours / [$XXX]) and timeline impact ([X] days) to incorporate [Stakeholder Name]'s requests without compromising build quality. I've attached the Change Order for your review—once signed, we will integrate these updates immediately. Best regards, [Your Name]
    Scenario 4

    "Polite pre-project scope boundary reminder"

    Include with your project kickoff email to set crystal-clear expectations early.

    Hi [Client Name], We're thrilled to kick off [Project Name]! To guarantee we hit our launch deadline of [Date] on budget, our team works strictly within the agreed Statement of Work. If any new creative ideas or feature requests pop up during the build (which happens often!), we'll capture them in our "Phase 2 Backlog" or provide an immediate Change Order estimate so you always maintain complete control over budget and timeline. Looking forward to building something extraordinary together! Best regards, [Your Name]
